Cal AI alternatives in 2026, compared.

Published June 9, 2026 · by the Eats AI team

Cal AI made photo calorie counting mainstream: point your camera at a plate and get calories and macros in seconds. But photo logging is where it stops. If you want help planning meals, cooking from what's in your fridge, managing grocery waste, or deciding what to order at a restaurant, you need a different app. Here are the six alternatives worth considering in 2026.

Transparency note: Eats AI is our app. We think it's the strongest alternative for most people, and we explain exactly why below, but we also tell you when another app is the better fit.

Why people switch from Cal AI

  • They want more than logging. Cal AI tells you what you ate. It does not plan what you'll eat next, manage your pantry, or generate recipes.
  • No help when eating out. Logging a burger after you ordered it is too late. None of the photo-counters help you choose the right dish in the first place.
  • No voice logging. Sometimes describing a meal is faster than photographing it.
  • Subscription value. If you are paying anyway, an app that replaces three or four other tools is a better deal than a single-purpose counter.

Consider something else if: you only want a bare-bones photo counter and nothing more. A single-purpose app may feel simpler.

2. MyFitnessPal: the database veteran

With 20+ million foods and barcode scanning, MyFitnessPal remains the deepest database in nutrition tracking, and its free tier is a real option. Logging is more manual than Cal AI or Eats AI, AI features sit behind premium, and there is no pantry, recipe generation, or dining-out help.

3. MacroFactor: the adaptive coach

MacroFactor's standout is its adherence-neutral algorithm that recalculates your targets weekly from your real weight trend. Serious lifters love it. It is not a photo-first app, so day-to-day logging takes more effort.

4. SnapCalorie: the free photo logger

SnapCalorie offers accurate photo and voice logging with micronutrient detail at no charge, making it the best free Cal AI replacement if logging is all you need. Planning, pantry, and restaurant features are absent.

5. Lose It!: the budget pick

Lose It! has photo logging, barcode scanning, and a friendly interface at a lower price point than most premium trackers, with a workable free tier. It covers the basics without the all-in-one depth.

6. Cronometer: the micronutrient specialist

If you track for medical reasons or want visibility into 80+ nutrients, Cronometer's data quality is the best available. It is detail-first rather than convenience-first, and most casual users find it more than they need.
